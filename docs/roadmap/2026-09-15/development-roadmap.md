# AI Security & Governance development roadmap

**Snapshot:** 15 September 2026  
**Purpose:** A directional development path for the public reference package. The sequence is a recommendation for learning and evidence, not a schedule or dependency chain.

## 1. Reference foundation

**Status:** Established and live

The public package now provides three peer security views and an AI Governance companion. Canonical sources, guides, maps, the editable presentation and the website form the shared reference foundation.

## 2. Application and CISO use

**Status:** Live

Applying Secure Business AI gives readers the complete six-stage journey. The CISO walkthrough turns Stage 0 Envision and the Stage 1 handoff into a short working conversation with reusable templates. Later project stages continue to use the full guide.

## 3. Confidence and reader learning

**Status:** Recommended next

Resolve the 30 original review findings, obtain human architectural review and test CISO and intended-reader comprehension. Then apply the supported public-writing cleanup and decide reuse terms. This work should precede expansion because it tests whether the current foundation is understood and defensible.

## 4. Scoped use and adaptation

**Status:** Future, scope and authorization required

Select one real workflow and environment for bounded exploration. Record what the use teaches, then revise the shared source only through review and a recorded decision. Downstream or audience-specific adaptations follow through the publication's intake contract and remain separate from the shared reference.

## Optional expansion from discussion

Additional bite-sized playbooks could deepen Stage 1 Discover and guardrail, then extend the current treatment into Pilot, Develop, Production transition and Operate. This is a proposal from discussion, not commissioned backlog work. It should be reconsidered after reader learning and a real-workflow exploration show where more guidance is useful.

## Interpretation

The four roadmap workstreams describe development of the publication. The six stages describe the journey of a business AI effort. They are different structures.

Arrows indicate the recommended order for learning. They do not imply that one workstream is a technical prerequisite for another, and they do not set delivery dates or commitments.
