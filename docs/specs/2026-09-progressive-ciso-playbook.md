# Progressive CISO playbook prototype

## Question and status

How can a CISO support the first conversation about a business AI idea, give useful early guidance without turning discovery into an approval exercise, and hand the effort into Discover and guardrail when the next experiment requires deeper work?

**Status:** Exploring. This specification authorizes a Stage 0 Envision and handoff prototype only. It does not revise the existing application companion, guides, site, PDFs, later stages, or accepted project intent.

## Starting point

The prototype starts from `content/guides/applying-secure-business-ai-guide.md`, especially Stage 0 Envision and Stage 1 Discover and guardrail, at content commit `bc74095c3fe9c3aa7a5765a90be1bebb4a77c022`. It also follows the business-led, proportional approach in `content/applying-secure-business-ai.md`.

The existing guide and its external review remain unchanged. This prototype is a proposed practical layer beneath those sources and does not inherit their review results.

## Proposed change

Create one compact, self-contained review draft in `docs/exploration/secure-business-ai-starter-playbook.md`. It should give the CISO a bite-sized Stage 0 view while preserving the larger model needed to support later stages.

The prototype must include:

- a short Stage 0 playbook covering what the team brings, what the CISO asks, guidance given now, what can wait, what security returns, and what triggers the Stage 1 handoff;
- a copyable starter questionnaire of about eight grouped questions;
- an initial qualitative risk view used in the first conversation and revisited at handoff;
- a copyable experiment-guidance template;
- a copyable Stage 1 handoff template;
- a worked customer-service credit-assistant example completed across all three templates;
- a clear distinction between what is needed now, what can wait, and what must exist before exposure expands;
- one living record that grows with the effort instead of a set of mandatory documents.

## Working principles

- The business leads the effort and owns the intended value. Security listens, helps the team see exposure, suggests safe ways to learn, operates assigned protections, and uses delegated authority where it exists.
- Stage position does not determine control depth. Data sensitivity, reach, actions, human effects, consequences, dependencies, and uncertainty determine it.
- Unknown is an acceptable first-conversation answer unless the unknown prevents a safe version of the contemplated experiment. In that case, narrow the experiment or use a safer alternative.
- Built, bought, embedded, and combined approaches receive only the questions relevant to them.
- The initial risk view is a first-conversation judgment, not a score, approval, or proof of assessment completeness.
- Stage 1 deepens only what the next bounded test needs. Real controls must be checked before the experiment uses actual data or can take actions.
- Production should meet enterprise expectations for its real exposure. Stage 0 does not require the team to design every production control.

## Evidence and counterevidence

The existing Stage 0 source calls for a short use-case note, preliminary data and action view, open questions, and receiving owners. Stage 1 calls for a shared factual picture, bounded guardrails, decision rights, and protection evidence. This supports a lightweight first conversation followed by targeted deepening.

The main counter-risk is that a short questionnaire could become a universal approval form or imply that unanswered questions are harmless. The prototype must therefore explain how to narrow experiments when uncertainty matters and keep decision authority with the people assigned to each question.

## Acceptance checks

- A business team can answer the starter questions without knowing its final architecture.
- The CISO can return useful guidance after one conversation without claiming approval or assessment completeness.
- The initial risk view records scenarios, consequences, exposure, important unknowns, recommendations, and owners without a numeric score.
- The credit-assistant example begins with no autonomous credit and no finance-system connection.
- The handoff asks Stage 1 to investigate only the facts and controls needed for the next bounded experiment.
- The playbook explains when an unknown requires a safer alternative.
- The prototype maps its outputs to the existing Stage 0 and Stage 1 guide fields.
- No later-stage content, site page, rendered asset, accepted decision, or intent record changes.

## Result that would support or reject the idea

The idea is supported if a reviewer can use the playbook to hold a short, enabling conversation, define a bounded next experiment, and identify the few questions Stage 1 must deepen without mistaking the record for approval. It should be revised or rejected if it creates routine document work, hides material exposure, assigns the CISO decisions held by others, or cannot adapt across buying, building, embedding, and combining AI.
